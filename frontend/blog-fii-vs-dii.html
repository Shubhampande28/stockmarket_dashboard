FII vs DII: Who Moves the Indian Stock Market?

Two acronyms show up in almost every Indian market report: FII and DII. Both are large institutional investors, but they behave very differently -- and watching how they disagree with each other is one of the simplest ways to understand what's really happening beneath a day's headline index move.

What is an FII?

A Foreign Institutional Investor (FII), also called a Foreign Portfolio Investor (FPI), is an overseas fund -- a pension fund, hedge fund, sovereign wealth fund, or asset manager -- registered to invest in Indian markets through a custodian bank. FIIs move large sums in and out of India based substantially on global conditions: US interest rates, the strength of the dollar, bond yields, and how attractive emerging markets look relative to developed ones at any given moment. Because that calculus can shift quickly, FII flows can swing from heavy buying to heavy selling within weeks.

What is a DII?

A Domestic Institutional Investor (DII) is an India-based institution -- mutual funds, insurance companies such as LIC, pension funds such as EPFO, and banks' treasury desks. A large and growing share of DII buying comes indirectly from millions of retail investors' monthly SIP (Systematic Investment Plan) contributions into mutual funds, which flow into the market every month regardless of short-term sentiment. That gives DII flows a steadier, more automatic character than FII flows.

Why they often move in opposite directions

In recent years, a recurring pattern has shown up repeatedly: FIIs sell heavily during periods of global risk-off sentiment, while DIIs -- backed by steady SIP inflows -- buy through the same period, cushioning the market. This is exactly why looking at only one side of the ledger can be misleading. A day with heavy FII selling can still see the index hold up if DII buying is strong enough to absorb it; a day with modest FII selling can hurt more if DII buying is weak that day too.

FII heavy selling + DII heavy buying

The most common "resilient market" pattern in recent years -- domestic flows absorbing foreign outflows.

FII buying + DII buying

Broad-based demand from both sides; usually accompanies strong up-moves.

Both selling

Rarer, and worth paying attention to -- it suggests both global and domestic sentiment have turned cautious at the same time.

Where to find the numbers

NSE publishes each day's net FII and DII cash-market activity, in ₹ crore, after market close. Equilytics pulls this directly from NSE's daily disclosure and shows it on the FII DII data page alongside a 30-day table and chart, so you can see the trend rather than just one day's number in isolation. The 5-session trailing sum of FII flows is also one of the six inputs to the Equilytics Mood Index.

A word of caution

FII and DII data describes what happened, not what will happen next. A single day's number, in either direction, says very little on its own -- it's the trend over several sessions that tends to matter more. Use this data as context for understanding a move you've already seen, not as a trigger for any specific trade.
